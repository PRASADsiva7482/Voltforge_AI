[Q] how to wire an LED [A] Wire GPIO -> 220 Ohm to 330 Ohm resistor -> LED anode, and LED cathode -> GND. Never connect an LED directly to a GPIO or supply rail without a resistor.
[Q] why use a resistor with LED [A] The resistor limits current. Use R = (supply voltage - LED forward voltage) / target current, then check resistor power with P = I^2 * R.
[Q] how to wire rgb led [A] Use one resistor per color channel. For common cathode, connect the common pin to GND and drive R/G/B pins from GPIO. For common anode, connect common to VCC and drive channels active-low.
[Q] how to wire neopixel [A] Connect VCC, GND, and DIN to one GPIO. For 5V NeoPixels from a 3.3V board, add a logic-level shifter and a bulk capacitor across the LED supply.
[Q] how to wire dht11 [A] Connect VCC, GND, and DATA to a digital GPIO. Add a 4.7 kOhm to 10 kOhm pull-up from DATA to VCC unless the DHT module already includes it.
[Q] how to wire dht22 [A] Connect VCC, GND, and DATA to a digital GPIO. Use the DHT22 type in firmware and keep sampling intervals slow enough for the sensor.
[Q] how to wire ultrasonic sensor [A] Connect VCC, GND, TRIG to an output GPIO, and ECHO to an input GPIO. On 3.3V boards, level-shift or divide the HC-SR04 ECHO signal if the module is powered from 5V.
[Q] how to wire pir sensor [A] Connect VCC, GND, and OUT to a digital input. Give the PIR module warm-up time and verify whether OUT is 3.3V-safe for the selected board.
[Q] how to connect ldr [A] Use the LDR with a fixed resistor as a voltage divider and connect the midpoint to an analog input. Raspberry Pi SBC boards need an external ADC for this.
[Q] how to wire potentiometer [A] Connect the two outer pins to VCC and GND, then connect the wiper to an analog input. Read it with analogRead on MCU boards that have ADC pins.
[Q] how to connect mpu6050 [A] MPU6050 uses I2C. Connect VCC, GND, SDA, and SCL to the board's I2C pins, and keep pull-ups at the board logic voltage.
[Q] how to connect lcd i2c [A] Connect LCD VCC and GND, then SDA/SCL to the board's I2C pins. Arduino Uno uses A4/A5, ESP32 commonly uses D21/D22, and Raspberry Pi 40-pin boards use GPIO2/GPIO3.
[Q] how to connect oled [A] Most SSD1306 OLED modules use I2C. Connect VCC, GND, SDA, and SCL, then use an SSD1306 display library and confirm the I2C address.
[Q] how to wire 7 segment display [A] Each LED segment needs current limiting. Match common cathode or common anode wiring and consider a driver IC for more than one digit.
[Q] how to connect relay [A] Connect relay-module VCC, GND, and IN to a GPIO. Wire the external load through COM and NO or NC. Many relay modules are active-low.
[Q] how to wire dc motor [A] Do not drive a DC motor directly from a GPIO. Use a MOSFET, transistor, relay module, or H-bridge driver, add flyback protection, and share ground.
[Q] how to connect servo [A] Connect signal to a PWM/timer-capable GPIO, VCC to a suitable 5V or 6V supply, and GND to the MCU ground. Use an external supply for larger servos.
[Q] how to wire stepper motor [A] Drive stepper coils through a driver board such as ULN2003, A4988, or DRV8825. The MCU should connect only to driver inputs, not directly to coils.
[Q] how to use buzzer [A] Active buzzers can be switched with digitalWrite. Passive buzzers need tone or PWM. Use a transistor driver if the current exceeds the GPIO budget.
[Q] how to use breadboard [A] Breadboard terminal rows are connected in small groups and power rails may be split. Add jumper wires across rail breaks when needed.
[Q] how to use ammeter [A] Place an ammeter in series with the branch you want to measure. Do not connect it directly across power and ground.
[Q] how to use oscilloscope [A] Connect scope CH1 or CH2 to the signal node and scope GND to circuit ground. A floating ground can produce misleading readings.
[Q] what is raspberry pi [A] Raspberry Pi can mean a Linux single-board computer such as Pi 4 or Pi 5, or a microcontroller board such as Pico. Linux Pi GPIO is 3.3V-only and has no analog input.
[Q] pi [A] If you mean Raspberry Pi, choose between Pico for microcontroller firmware projects and Pi 3/4/5/Zero for Linux GPIO projects. Both use 3.3V GPIO; Pi SBCs need an external ADC for analog sensors.
[Q] raspberry pi processor [A] Raspberry Pi processors depend on model: Pico uses RP2040/RP2350 class microcontrollers, Pi 4 uses Broadcom BCM2711, and Pi 5 uses Broadcom BCM2712.
[Q] raspberry pi gpio voltage [A] Raspberry Pi 40-pin GPIO is 3.3V-only. Do not connect 5V sensor outputs directly to GPIO; use a divider or level shifter.
[Q] raspberry pi i2c pins [A] On Raspberry Pi 40-pin boards, I2C1 is commonly GPIO2 for SDA and GPIO3 for SCL. In VoltForge these appear as D2 and D3.
[Q] raspberry pi analog input [A] Raspberry Pi 3/4/5/Zero boards do not have built-in analog inputs. Use an external ADC such as MCP3008 or ADS1115 for LDRs, potentiometers, and analog sensors.
[Q] raspberry pi pico processor [A] Raspberry Pi Pico uses the RP2040 dual-core Arm Cortex-M0+ microcontroller. Pico 2 uses the newer RP2350 class microcontroller.
[Q] arduino uno processor [A] Arduino Uno R3 uses the ATmega328P AVR microcontroller at 16 MHz with 5V GPIO logic.
[Q] esp32 pinout [A] ESP32 uses 3.3V GPIO, commonly I2C SDA on D21 and SCL on D22. Avoid strapping pins for circuits that affect boot, and avoid ADC2 pins when Wi-Fi analog reading matters.
[Q] stm32 blue pill processor [A] STM32 Blue Pill commonly uses an STM32F103C8 Arm Cortex-M3 microcontroller at 72 MHz with 3.3V GPIO.
[Q] teensy 4 processor [A] Teensy 4.0 and 4.1 use the NXP i.MX RT1062 Arm Cortex-M7 at 600 MHz with 3.3V GPIO.
